# Outreach Templates — B-Acoustics

Reference templates for the user's own use (email/text/LinkedIn) — not
website content, not deployed anywhere. Two sets below: (1) referral
outreach to architects and contractors, per `ROADMAP.md`'s Phase 2b, and
(2) a simplified client follow-up template, replacing one that client
feedback flagged as too complex/offputting.

---

## 1. Referral partnership outreach — architects & contractors

**Positioning note:** lean on the site's own framing — "architects and
acoustic engineers, at the same table" (`index.html`'s Approach section).
The pitch isn't "send me your overflow work," it's "bring me in early on
projects with an acoustic dimension, the same way you'd loop in a
structural engineer" — collaborator framing, not vendor framing. Keep it
short; a long pitch reads as a sales email, and architects/contractors get
plenty of those.

> **Subject:** Acoustic input on [Firm]'s upcoming projects

> before drawings are finalized is often enough to avoid a costly retrofit
> later.

> if useful, or just keep this for when it's relevant.
>

### D. LinkedIn connection note (300-char limit — keep this tight)

> Hi [Name] — I run B-Acoustics, acoustic engineering/soundproofing for
> fit-outs in Singapore. Would be great to connect, especially if
> acoustic scope ever comes up on your projects (party walls, studios,
> office fit-outs, noise compliance).

### Usage notes

- Personalize the opening line every time — a generic-sounding cold email
  to a design/build firm reads as spam immediately. Reference an actual
  project, event, or mutual contact wherever possible.
- Don't propose a formal referral-fee arrangement in the first message —
  raise it only after a real relationship exists (second or third
  conversation), if at all. Leading with money in a cold email undercuts
  the "collaborator, not vendor" positioning.
- Track who you've sent this to and follow up once, politely, after
  2-3 weeks of silence — don't chase further than that.

## 2. Simplified client follow-up template

**Context:** the website's enquiry form already collects name, company,
email, phone, a setting type (home/office/industrial), and a free-text
description of the space. The complaint is about what happens *after*
that — a follow-up email/text asking for more scoping detail before a
quote, which some clients found too complex/off-putting.

**I don't have the current version of that follow-up to diff against** — the
templates below are a from-scratch simplified version, built on one
principle: **ask only what's needed to book a site visit or give a rough
ballpark; defer the rest to that visit/call.** B-Acoustics already doesn't
quote firm pricing without a site survey, so a long pre-visit questionnaire
is mostly redundant effort for the client — everything technical gets
re-confirmed on-site anyway. If the old template asked for things not
covered here (e.g. specific measurements, existing wall material), that's
exactly the kind of detail worth cutting and deferring to the visit instead
of restoring.

### Email version

> Subject: Re: Your enquiry — quick next step
>
> Hi [Name],
>
> Thanks for reaching out about [the space/project, e.g. "your home
> theatre"]. To get you a rough idea and set up a site visit, just three
> quick things:
>
> 1. **Best day/time** for a short site visit (15-20 min)?
> 2. **Rough timeline** — is this urgent, or a few months out?
> 3. Anything else on your mind — budget range, or a specific concern
>    (e.g. "sound getting in" vs. "sound getting out")? Totally fine to
>    skip this if you'd rather just talk it through in person.
>
> That's it — everything else (wall type, exact dimensions, target rating)
> is easier to sort out on-site than over email anyway.
>
> Looking forward to it,
> [Name]
> B-Acoustics

### WhatsApp/SMS version (shorter still)

> Hi [Name], thanks for the enquiry! To set up a quick site visit — what
> day/time works, and is this urgent or a few months out? We'll sort the
> technical details in person. — B-Acoustics

### Usage notes

- **Three questions, max, in the first follow-up.** If more detail is
  genuinely needed before a visit can even be scheduled (rare), ask it as
  a fourth bullet, not a numbered list beyond 3-4 items — past that, it
  starts reading as a form to fill out rather than a conversation.
- **Make the "optional" parts visibly optional** ("totally fine to skip
  this") — clients disengage from anything that feels like mandatory
  homework before they've even spoken to a person.
- **Lead with scheduling, not scoping.** The fastest way to lower
  perceived complexity is to make the ask "when can we talk" rather than
  "describe your technical requirements" — the latter is what a client
  isn't equipped to answer precisely anyway, which is likely the real
  source of the "off-putting" feedback.
- If you have the actual old template handy, share it and I'll do a
  direct before/after diff instead of a from-scratch rewrite — that'll be
  more precise about which specific questions triggered the pushback.
