HDB vs condo soundproofing rules in Singapore

The acoustic problem is often similar — a shared wall, a noisy floor above, a home theatre that needs isolating. The approval process to actually do the work is not. Here's how HDB and condo rules actually differ.

A

HDB: permits, timing, and no fixed acoustic spec

HDB renovation work — including the flooring and wall build-ups that soundproofing typically involves — requires a permit applied for through HDB's electronic APEX system, submitted by a contractor listed in HDB's Directory of Renovation Contractors (DRC). Removing existing floor finishes or raising floor levels are both permit-triggering works; overlaying new flooring on top of existing tiles is generally permitted, while hacking tiles requires the waterproofing and screed to be reinstated afterward.

Timing is tightly controlled: general renovation work is typically allowed 9am-6pm on weekdays and Saturdays, noisy work such as hacking and drilling is usually restricted to weekdays only (commonly 9am-5pm), a daily quiet hour applies from 1-2pm, and no renovation work — noisy or otherwise — is permitted on Sundays or public holidays.

What HDB does not do is mandate a specific acoustic rating (an STC or impact-noise figure) for flooring or party-wall build-ups. The permit process governs what work is allowed and when — the actual soundproofing performance of what gets installed is a specification decision, not a fixed number HDB checks against.

B

Condo: MCST approval, not HDB rules

None of the HDB permit or timing framework above applies to condos. A condo's Management Corporation Strata Title (MCST) governs renovation approval instead, through its own house rules — typically covering permitted renovation hours, a security deposit, contractor registration with the management office, and sign-off before work affecting common property or shared structure (which a party wall usually is) can begin.

MCST rules vary by development — there's no single Singapore-wide standard the way there is for HDB permits — so the first step on any condo acoustic project is confirming the specific development's house rules, not assuming they match another condo you've worked with before.

C

What this means for your project

For an HDB flat, the practical sequence is: agree the acoustic target first, then have your DRC-listed contractor apply for the permit with that build-up specified, scheduling noisy work inside the allowed weekday hours. For a condo, it's: get the MCST's house rules and approval requirements up front, since they determine both the timeline and what's structurally permitted (some MCSTs restrict works to the party wall or floor slab more tightly than HDB does).

In both cases, the acoustic assessment and target-setting is the same discipline — a baseline reading, a target STC or impact-noise figure, and a build-up sized to hit it. What changes is only the paperwork and scheduling around getting the physical work approved.

The short version: HDB controls when and how you can renovate, but not what soundproofing rating you install. Condo MCSTs control both, and their rules differ development to development — confirm them before setting a project timeline.

Frequently asked

Does HDB have a minimum sound-insulation spec for flooring?

No codified sound-insulation rating is mandated for HDB flooring works. HDB provides renovation guidance and permit requirements, but the acoustic performance of any flooring build-up is a specification choice, not a fixed regulatory number — which is exactly where an acoustic assessment adds value.

Can I do noisy renovation work on a Sunday in an HDB flat?

No. Noisy work such as hacking and drilling is restricted to weekdays, and no renovation work is permitted on Sundays or public holidays, with a daily quiet hour also in effect.

Who approves soundproofing work in a condo — HDB or the MCST?

Neither HDB rule applies to condos. Condo renovation and any work affecting shared structure or common property goes through the development's Management Corporation Strata Title (MCST), which sets its own house rules and approval process.

Do I need a licensed contractor for HDB soundproofing work?

Renovation permits must be applied for by a contractor listed in HDB's Directory of Renovation Contractors (DRC) — this applies to the works themselves, separate from any acoustic consultancy advising on the specification.
