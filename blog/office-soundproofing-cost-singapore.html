What actually drives the cost of office soundproofing

Every fit-out enquiry starts with the same question: how much will this cost? The honest answer is that office soundproofing is priced on a handful of variables, not a flat rate per square metre. Understanding them helps you brief a contractor accurately and avoid scope surprises later.

1. What's already there

A lightweight drywall partition, a solid masonry wall, and an exposed concrete slab all start from a different baseline. Retrofitting mass or damping onto an existing lightweight partition — resilient channels, additional layers of drywall, mass-loaded vinyl — takes more labour and material than treating a surface that's already dense. The condition of the base build is usually the single biggest swing factor in any quote.

2. The acoustic rating you actually need

Two different metrics get specified for two different problems:

  • Sound Transmission Class (STC) — how well a wall, door, or floor/ceiling assembly blocks sound from moving between two rooms. This is what matters for boardroom confidentiality or a party wall between tenants.
  • Noise Reduction Coefficient (NRC) — how much a surface material absorbs sound within the same room, reducing echo and reverberation. This is what matters for an open-plan floor that feels loud and chaotic.

A higher target STC generally means a heavier, more layered assembly — which costs more in both material and installation time. A higher NRC target is usually about surface area of absorptive material, which scales more predictably. (We go deeper on this distinction in STC vs NRC, explained.)

3. What the room is actually used for

A single enclosed meeting room needing speech privacy is a contained, well-defined scope. An open-plan floor with a general noise complaint often needs a mixed approach — ceiling baffles, wall panels, and sometimes flooring changes — because the noise source and the annoyance point aren't the same desk. Diagnosing the actual use case before pricing avoids over- or under-specifying the fix.

4. Area, geometry, and penetrations

Total wall, ceiling, and floor area sets the base quantity of material. Less obvious cost drivers include ceiling height, the number of doors and windows that need acoustic seals, and services — ducting, cable trays, sprinkler penetrations — that break up an otherwise continuous assembly and need to be detailed around individually.

5. Material and finish

Fabric-wrapped panels, PET felt, mineral wool board, and acoustic plaster all perform differently and carry different material costs — and the finish quality expected in a client-facing boardroom is usually a different spec than a back-of-house plant room.

6. Installation constraints

Working in an occupied office, around a tenancy's after-hours access rules, or with MCST / building management approvals in a Singapore commercial building all add coordination time that shows up in the final price — independent of the materials themselves.

7. Whether the project needs sign-off

Some projects — particularly industrial or F&B fit-outs subject to noise guidelines — need a documented acoustic assessment or post-installation testing, not just the physical works. That engineering and verification step is a separate line item from installation.

In short: the cost of office soundproofing scales with how much mass and layering the target rating demands, not with floor area alone. Two rooms of identical size can land at very different price points depending on what's already built and what result is actually required.

Frequently asked

Does floor area alone determine soundproofing cost?

No — floor area sets a base quantity, but the existing base build condition and the target acoustic rating (STC or NRC) matter more than square footage alone.

Why do two similar-sized offices get very different quotes?

Because cost scales with how much mass and layering the target rating demands — a lightweight partition needing a high STC costs far more to retrofit than a solid wall needing the same rating.

Does an office soundproofing project always need acoustic testing?

Only some projects — particularly industrial or F&B fit-outs subject to noise guidelines — require a documented assessment or post-installation verification test, as a separate line item from the physical works.

What increases installation cost beyond materials?

Working around an occupied office's after-hours access rules, MCST or building management approvals, and detailing around existing services such as ducting and sprinklers all add coordination time reflected in the final price.
